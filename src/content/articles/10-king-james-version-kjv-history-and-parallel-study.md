## The Making of the King James Bible: Hampton Court to 1611

To appreciate the translation, one must understand the fraught political and religious atmosphere of seventeenth-century England.

### 1. The Hampton Court Conference (1604)
Following the death of Queen Elizabeth I in 1603, King James VI of Scotland ascended the English throne as King James I. England was religiously divided between two competing factions:
- The **Anglican Hierarchy:** Favored the episcopal structure and used the official **Bishops' Bible** (1568), which was widely viewed as pedantic and unpopular among the common people.
- The **Puritans:** Strongly reformed pastors and scholars who favored the beloved **Geneva Bible** (1560). The Geneva Bible, translated by Marian exiles in Switzerland, featured brilliant scholarship and was the Bible of William Shakespeare and the common home. However, it contained marginal study notes that fiercely challenged the "divine right of kings" and criticized tyrannical monarchs.

In January 1604, King James convened the Hampton Court Conference to address Puritan grievances. During the conference, Dr. John Reynolds, the Puritan President of Corpus Christi College, Oxford, proposed a bold compromise: **a new, uniform English translation of the Bible** that would unite the nation, free from partisan marginal political notes, to be read in all churches. King James enthusiastically approved the proposal.

### 2. The Forty-Seven Translators and the Six "Companies"
The translation work began in 1604 and was organized with unprecedented administrative and scholarly rigor. The forty-seven translators were divided into **six distinct committees (companies)** working across three prestigious academic centers:
- **Westminster Old Testament Company:** Translated Genesis through 2 Kings. Led by the linguistic genius Lancelot Andrewes, who was master of fifteen ancient and modern languages.
- **Cambridge Old Testament Company:** Translated 1 Chronicles through the Song of Solomon.
- **Oxford Old Testament Company:** Translated Isaiah through Malachi.
- **Oxford New Testament Company:** Translated the four Gospels, Acts, and Revelation. Led by John Perin and Thomas Ravis.
- **Westminster New Testament Company:** Translated the Epistles of Paul, Peter, James, John, and Jude.
- **Cambridge Apocrypha Company:** Translated the historical Deuterocanonical and Apocryphal books.

### 3. The Unprecedented Review Process (The Fifteen Rules)
King James issued fifteen strict translation rules to govern the companies. Crucially, Rule 8 mandated that:
> *"Every particular man of each company, was to take the same chapter of chapters, and having translated or amended them severally by himself, where he thought good, all to meet together, confer what they had done, and agree for their Parts what should stand."*

Once a company completed a book, it was sent to all the other five companies for critical review! Finally, a select committee of twelve senior scholars (two from each company) met at Stationers' Hall in London for nine consecutive months, reviewing the entire manuscript word by word and reading it aloud to test its acoustic cadence and liturgical ear. When the 1611 King James Bible was issued from the royal press of Robert Barker, it represented the most thoroughly scrutinized translation in human history.

### The Acoustic Testing at Stationers' Hall: Engineered for the Ear

One of the least understood secrets behind the enduring power of the King James Bible is that it was not designed merely to be read silently with the eye; it was deliberately **engineered for the human ear**.

In seventeenth-century England, the vast majority of ordinary citizens were illiterate or possessed limited reading skills. Their primary encounter with scripture occurred inside stone parish churches, where the parish priest or curate stood at the lectern and read the daily morning and evening prayer lessons aloud from the Great Chained Bible.

The King James translation committee was keenly aware of this acoustic reality. When the twelve senior delegates gathered at Stationers' Hall in London between 1609 and 1610 for the final revision phase, they instituted an extraordinary procedure: one scholar sat at the center of the hall with the master manuscript and read every single verse aloud at full volume, while the other eleven scholars sat around him holding competing Greek, Hebrew, Latin, French, and earlier English texts.

If a sentence caused the reader to stumble, if the consonants clashed harshly, or if the rhythmic meter lacked liturgical balance, the translation was paused and debated. They adjusted word choices not simply for semantic precision, but for **cadence, poetic meter, and acoustic resonance**:
- Notice the rhythmic, iambic majesty of Genesis 1:3: *"And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."*
- Compare this with the clunky, staccato cadences of modern colloquial translations.
- Notice the soaring crescendo of Romans 8:38–39: *"For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."*

### The Royal Rules: Ecclesiastical Continuity & The Banishment of Partisan Notes

Another vital historical reality often forgotten today is the role of King James's **Fifteen Royal Injunctions**.

The King specifically commanded:
- **Rule 1:** *"The ordinary Bible read in the Church, commonly called the Bishops Bible, to be followed, and as little altered as the Truth of the original will permit."* (This ensured continuity with the existing liturgical memory of the English church).
- **Rule 3:** *"The Old Ecclesiastical Words to be kept, viz. the Word Church not to be translated Congregation &c."* (King James was fiercely opposed to the radical Puritans who wished to eliminate historic terms like *church*, *bishop*, and *charity* in favor of anti-hierarchical language).
- **Rule 6:** *"No Marginal Notes at all to be affixed, but only for the explanation of the Hebrew or Greek words, which cannot without some circumlocution, so briefly and fitly be expressed in the Text."*

This sixth rule proved to be a stroke of providential genius. By strictly prohibiting doctrinal, political, or sectarian commentary in the margins (unlike the Geneva Bible, whose inflammatory notes attacked secular rulers), the King James text stood clean, majestic, and universally acceptable across divergent theological factions. The Word of God was permitted to speak for itself, unencumbered by human partisan agendas.

## The Underlying Manuscripts: The Textus Receptus Debate

A central reason why the King James Bible continues to inspire fierce devotion in 2026 is its underlying manuscript foundation: the **Textus Receptus (Received Text)**.

### 1. The Byzantine Text Tradition and the Textus Receptus
The New Testament of the King James Bible was not translated from Latin; it was translated directly from the Greek text compiled during the Renaissance by Desiderius Erasmus (1516), later refined by Robert Estienne (Stephanus, 1550) and Theodore Beza (1598). 

This printed Greek text was derived from the **Byzantine manuscript family**—the vast majority (over 85%) of surviving Greek manuscripts preserved through centuries of Byzantine scribal copying in the Greek-speaking Eastern church. Proponents of the Received Text emphasize that God in His providential preservation did not allow the true text of scripture to remain buried in Egyptian sand for fifteen centuries, but kept it continuous, living, and public in the majority of historical manuscripts.

### 2. The Rise of the Alexandrian Critical Text
In the late nineteenth century, scholars like B. F. Westcott and F. J. A. Hort championed a different approach. They argued that older manuscripts—specifically **Codex Sinaiticus** and **Codex Vaticanus**, dating to the fourth century AD and originating from Alexandria, Egypt—were superior because they were chronologically closer to the apostolic autographs.

Modern translations (such as the ESV, NIV, and NASB) follow the eclectic **Critical Text** (Nestle-Aland 28th / UBS 5th edition), which omits or brackets several famous passages found in the KJV, including:
- The Longer Ending of Mark (Mark 16:9–20)
- The Woman Caught in Adultery (*Pericope Adulterae*, John 7:53–8:11)
- The Johannine Comma (1 John 5:7–8)

## Why the KJV's Grammatical Precision (Thou vs. Ye) Still Matters

Modern readers often view the King James pronouns—*thou*, *thee*, *thy*, *thine*, *ye*, and *you*—as mere decorative archaic flourishes. However, in 1611, these pronouns served a vital **grammatical distinction** that modern English has completely lost!

In 1611 English:
- **Thou / Thee / Thy / Thine:** Strictly **singular** (referring to one individual).
  - *Thou* is the singular subject (*"Thou art the man"*).
  - *Thee* is the singular object (*"I love thee"*).
- **Ye / You / Your / Yours:** Strictly **plural** (referring to a group, assembly, or nation).
  - *Ye* is the plural subject (*"Ye are the light of the world"*).
  - *You* is the plural object (*"I speak unto you"*).

### The Theological Significance in John 3:7:
In Jesus's midnight discourse with Nicodemus in John 3:7, the King James Version reads:
> *"Marvel not that I said unto **thee** [singular], **Ye** [plural] must be born again."*

Notice the staggering theological precision captured by the KJV:
- Jesus says unto ***thee*** (singular, Nicodemus): Do not be astonished that I told you personally...
- That ***ye*** (plural, all humanity): All people everywhere must be born from above!

In modern English, where the single word "you" is used for both singular and plural, this crucial distinction is completely invisible unless one looks at the Greek text. The King James Version preserves the exact numerical distinction of the original Greek with infallible consistency.
