## The Architectural Anatomy of a Strong's Number

To use Strong's Concordance effectively, one must understand what a Strong's number actually represents—and what it does not represent.

### 1. Root Lemma vs. Inflected Surface Form
Biblical Hebrew and Koine Greek are **inflected languages**. In English, grammatical relationships are largely indicated through word order and auxiliary prepositions (e.g., *"to the house"*, *"from the house"*). In ancient Greek and Hebrew, grammatical relationships are indicated by altering the prefix, vowel pointing, or suffix of the word itself.

For example, the Greek verb for "to love" in its lexical dictionary form (lemma) is **ἀγαπάω** (*agapaō* - Strong’s **G25**). However, across the New Testament, this single verb appears in dozens of distinct inflected surface forms depending on its grammatical role:
- *Agapāi* (He loves - 3rd person singular present active indicative)
- *Agapōmen* (Let us love - 1st person plural present active subjunctive)
- *Ēgapēsen* (He loved - 3rd person singular aorist active indicative, as in John 3:16)
- *Agapēte* (Beloved - vocative plural adjective)

Dr. James Strong’s ingenious insight was to assign the number **G25** not to every grammatical variation, but to the **root dictionary lemma** (*agapaō*). When you tap the word "loved" in John 3:16 on AduBible, Strong's number **G25** connects you directly to the timeless foundational meaning of divine, unconditional sacrificial love, while the morphological parser informs you that the verb is an **Aorist Active Indicative**, signifying a decisive, historically accomplished act of divine self-giving on Calvary.

### 2. The Hebrew Numbering System (H1 – H8674)
The Old Testament concordance spans **8,674 entries**. 
- **Entries H1 through H8674** cover Biblical Hebrew and biblical Imperial Aramaic (the language found in portions of Daniel 2:4–7:28, Ezra 4:8–6:18; 7:12–26, and Jeremiah 10:11).
- Entry **H1** begins with *'āb* (father, ancestor, patriarch).
- Entry **H8674** concludes with *Tatnay* (Tattenai, a Persian governor in Ezra).
- Key landmark Hebrew numbers include:
  - **H430:** *Elohim* (God, the Supreme Creator, plural of majesty).
  - **H3068:** *YHWH / Yahweh* (The self-existent covenant Lord, rendered in all-caps "LORD" in English Bibles).
  - **H7965:** *Shalom* (Completeness, peace, welfare, soundness, tranquility).
  - **H2617:** *Hesed* (Steadfast covenant love, unfailing lovingkindness, mercy).

### 3. The Greek Numbering System (G1 – G5624)
The New Testament concordance spans **5,624 entries**.
- Entry **G1** begins with the first letter of the Greek alphabet: *Alpha* (Α), symbolizing Christ as the eternal beginning of all things (Revelation 1:8).
- Entry **G5624** concludes with *ōphelimos* (profitable, advantageous, spiritually beneficial, as in 2 Timothy 3:16).
- Key landmark Greek numbers include:
  - **G3056:** *Logos* (The Divine Word, Reason, Expression; in John 1:1, Jesus Christ as the eternal revelation of the Father).
  - **G26:** *Agape* (Unconditional, self-sacrificing divine love).
  - **G5485:** *Charis* (Grace, unmerited divine favor, spiritual enablement).
  - **G4151:** *Pneuma* (Spirit, breath, wind; specifically the Holy Spirit).
  - **G4102:** *Pistis* (Faith, divine persuasion, firm conviction of God's truth).

## Five Golden Rules of Biblical Word Studies (Avoiding Exegetical Fallacies)

In his landmark academic work *Exegetical Fallacies*, New Testament scholar D. A. Carson warned that while concordances are among the greatest gifts to biblical scholarship, they can also become dangerous instruments when used carelessly. Misinterpreting a Greek or Hebrew root word can lead to distorted theology. 

To conduct sound, scholarly word studies on AduBible, always adhere to these five golden hermeneutical principles:

### 1. Avoid the "Root Fallacy" (Etymological Fallacy)
The root fallacy is the mistaken assumption that a word’s true meaning is always determined by the sum of its historic physical roots. 
- *A Classic Example:* In 2 Corinthians 9:7, Paul writes that *"God loves a cheerful giver."* The Greek word for "cheerful" is *hilaros* (**G2431**), from which the English word "hilarious" is derived. An untrained preacher might proclaim: *"God wants you to laugh uncontrollably while giving your offering!"* This is an etymological fallacy. In 1st-century Koine Greek, *hilaros* simply meant glad, willing, and free from grudging reluctance. Modern English derivatives do not dictate ancient Greek semantics.
- *How to Prevent It:* Always prioritize how the biblical author uses the word in its immediate literary context, rather than relying on its etymological roots.

### 2. Context Determines Meaning, Not the Dictionary List
When you open a Strong's entry on AduBible, you will often see multiple English definitions listed in Thayer's or Gesenius's lexicon. It is a grave error to assume that *all* of those definitions apply every time the word appears.
- For example, the Greek word *sarx* (**G4561**) can mean:
  1. The physical body/flesh of a human being (John 1:14: *"The Word became flesh"*).
  2. The fallen, sinful human nature prone to rebellion against God (Romans 8:8: *"Those who are in the flesh cannot please God"*).
  3. Humanity in general (Luke 3:6: *"All flesh shall see the salvation of God"*).
- If you import the definition of "sinful nature" into John 1:14, you would fall into the blasphemous heresy of believing Christ possessed a sinful human nature! Context alone tells you which lexical sense the inspired author intended.

### 3. Beware of "Anachronistic Semantic Overload"
Do not read modern scientific, philosophical, or psychological definitions back into ancient biblical terms.
- In Romans 1:16, Paul writes that the gospel is the *"power of God to salvation."* The Greek word for power is *dynamis* (**G1411**). Preachers often announce: *"The gospel is dynamite!"* While dramatic, dynamite was invented by Alfred Nobel in 1867 as an explosive agent of physical destruction. In the ancient world, *dynamis* meant inherent capability, strength, and life-producing divine energy. The gospel does not blow people to pieces; it raises spiritually dead sinners to life.

### 4. Recognize Author-Specific Vocabularies
Different biblical writers used vocabulary in distinct ways. 
- The Apostle Paul frequently uses the word *dikaioō* (**G1344**) in a legal, forensic sense: to be *declared righteous* before God's tribunal by faith alone, apart from works of the law (Romans 3:28).
- The Apostle James uses *dikaioō* in an evidentiary, demonstrative sense: to be *proven or shown to be righteous* before watching men by the living fruit of good works (James 2:24).
- When doing a word study, examine how that specific author (Paul, John, Luke, or Peter) employs the term across their own epistles before harmonizing it across the wider canon.

### 5. Cross-Examine Occurrences Across Multiple Translations
Never conduct a word study in a vacuum. Utilize AduBible’s **4-column parallel view** to see how the word is translated across formal translations (NKJV, KJV), optimal modern editions (BSB), and indigenous mother tongues (Asante Twi). Often, observing how another language translates a Greek word uncovers a profound conceptual angle that your native tongue obscured.

### The Critical Synergy of Strong's Numbers and Morphological Parsing

A common question among growing students of scripture is: *"If I have Strong's numbers, why do I also need morphological parsing?"*

The answer lies in the dynamic nature of biblical languages. A Strong's number provides the **static root identity** (the dictionary definition) of a word, while **morphological parsing reveals its dynamic operational role** in the sentence. 

Consider Ephesians 2:8:
> *"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God..."*

When you click on the word **saved** on AduBible, the popover provides two vital layers of insight:
1. **Strong’s G4982:** *sōzō* — to rescue, deliver, heal, preserve from eternal destruction, bring into safety.
2. **Morphological Tag:** **V-RPP-NPM** (Verb, Perfect Passive Participle, Nominative Plural Masculine).

The grammatical tense here is **Perfect Passive**. In Greek grammar:
- The **Passive Voice** proves that salvation is not something we accomplish for ourselves; we are the passive recipients of God’s monergistic sovereign grace.
- The **Perfect Tense** signifies an action completed in past historical time that possesses permanent, ongoing, unbroken results in the present! 

Paul does not say *"you are being tentatively saved today if you maintain good behavior"* (Present tense), nor does he say *"you were once saved in the past, but the status has lapsed"* (Aorist tense). He uses the Perfect tense: at the moment of regeneration in Christ, you were decisively saved, and you stand permanently in that state of accomplished salvation today!

Without morphological parsing, a reader only knows that *sōzō* means "to save." With AduBible’s integrated morphological engine, the full eternal security and sovereign assurance of God’s covenant grace explodes with crystalline certainty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What do the letters "H" and "G" mean in Strong's numbers?
In Strong’s Exhaustive Concordance, the letter **"H"** indicates an Old Testament Biblical Hebrew or Aramaic root lemma (ranging from H1 to H8674). The letter **"G"** indicates a New Testament Koine Greek root lemma (ranging from G1 to G5624).
